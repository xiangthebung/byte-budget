Byte Budget

Two questions, then it runs on its own

Byte Budget is already counting. Answering these turns a running total into a position — on track, or not. All of it is optional; skip it and nothing breaks.

How big is your plan, and when does it reset?

What saving a plan does

The size becomes an allowance over everything you browse, and that allowance is what the warnings are measured against. They arrive at 75%, 90% and 100%. Those three are fixed; there is no fourth.

Nothing is refused below 100%. At 100% Byte Budget starts refusing subresources — images, video, scripts — on every site until the allowance rolls over. The page itself always loads, carrying a banner that says why. Change the allowance, pause it for an hour, or remove it entirely in Settings.

Reset days stop at the 28th because every month has one. A cycle anchored to the 31st would reset on a different date in February, and the reset date is the one figure here you check against a bill.

Optional

Data Saver

Asks known image CDNs for a smaller version of the same picture, refuses analytics beacons, and stops video that is off screen from pre-buffering. The individual features, per-site exceptions and the off switch are all in Settings.

To measure what that saves rather than estimate it, about one page load in ten is deliberately left unoptimized as a control.

What these numbers do not cover

Everything Byte Budget counts happens inside this Chrome profile. A second profile, a different browser, apps outside Chrome, system and app updates, and every other device sharing your connection are invisible to an extension — there is no API that would show them — so a carrier’s figure can sit well above this one, sometimes by a factor of several. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account.
