# Byte Budget — Audit

## 1. Verdict

This is a finished, unusually honest measurement engine wrapped in a product that has not yet been connected to the question it exists to answer. The measurement layer earns the README's claims: the three-source waterfall (`Content-Length` → `transferSize` → learned mean) is real, `estimatedDown` is threaded end-to-end so the measured share is computed rather than asserted, the holdout is a genuine randomised control that produces a subtraction instead of a model output, and the smoke test uses the local server and Playwright's route handler as the witness rather than the extension's own arithmetic — `770,830 served / 771,820 counted` is the kind of number most trackers cannot produce.

Two things hold it back. The first is a defect: DNR session rules are wiped on every service-worker wake and never republished, so a site parked at `strict` stops being enforced permanently after the first 30-second idle gap while every surface keeps saying it is limited ([src/rules/session.ts:83](src/rules/session.ts:83), [src/background.ts:104](src/background.ts:104)). The second, and the bigger one, is that an extraordinary amount of correct machinery is unreachable. Week/month/session budgets, the `hard` shape, `kbps`, all five site packs, five default-on page optimizers, `holdoutPercent`, `retentionDays`, `trackHosts`, `savedMeasured`, `GET_STORAGE_REPORT`, `pageScriptError()`, `clearEnforcement()`, `deltaTotal` and `generatedAt` are all implemented, tested in some cases, shipped to the UI in some cases, and reachable by no user. Meanwhile the one thing a person on a metered plan needs — "10 GB a month, resets on the 17th" — cannot be expressed at all, because `Budget` requires a site key ([src/limit/budgets.ts:39](src/limit/budgets.ts:39)) and `Settings` has no plan field ([src/core/types.ts:298](src/core/types.ts:298)).

The engineering is ahead of the product, and the product is ahead of its own UI.

## 2. Ship blockers

Eight, in the order they would sink a submission or a first user.

1. **The privacy policy describes controls the build cannot expose.** `normalize()` rebuilds `Settings` from defaults and carries forward only `theme` and `badge` ([src/core/settings.ts:25](src/core/settings.ts:25)), so `retentionDays` is permanently 400 and `trackHosts` permanently true. PRIVACY_POLICY.md:16 says host counts are "Switchable off in settings"; :30-31 says retention follows "your retention setting"; README.md:161-163 repeats both. Every user gets 400 days of per-site, per-host and per-page-load rows with no way to shorten it. A privacy policy that documents a control the product lacks is a takedown risk independent of anything technical. Either whitelist the two fields in `normalize()` and add the two controls to the Privacy & data panel (the worker already honours both — [src/background.ts:240](src/background.ts:240), [src/background.ts:307](src/background.ts:307)), or delete the claims. **High**

2. **Enforcement is silently switched off by MV3 worker teardown.** `bySource` is plain module state ([src/rules/session.ts:54](src/rules/session.ts:54)); `install()` removes every session rule Chrome holds and re-adds only what the warm cache composes ([src/rules/session.ts:88](src/rules/session.ts:88)). `applyOptimize()` runs at module top level on every wake ([src/background.ts:104](src/background.ts:104)), and with the shipping default `enabled: false` it composes zero rules — so a fresh worker's first act is to delete every limit rule and install nothing. Nothing republishes: `ensureEnforcementReady()` restores the decision map without calling `syncRules()` ([src/limit/enforce.ts:37](src/limit/enforce.ts:37)), and `syncEnforcement` then finds `wanted === enforcementFor(site)` and skips `setEnforcement` ([src/limit/governor.ts:151](src/limit/governor.ts:151)). It self-heals only when the tab set for a site changes ([src/limit/enforce.ts:131](src/limit/enforce.ts:131)) — a same-tab reload leaves it dead. Fix: `await syncRules()` at the end of the loading IIFE in `ensureEnforcementReady`, and await it before the first `applyOptimize()`. **High**

3. **Removing a budget leaves its block rules installed for the rest of the session.** `reloadBudgets` drops the site from `live` ([src/limit/governor.ts:75](src/limit/governor.ts:75)) and `syncEnforcement` iterates only `live` ([src/limit/governor.ts:145](src/limit/governor.ts:145)), so `setEnforcement(site, "off")` is never called. The `initiatorDomains` rule needs no open tab ([src/limit/rules.ts:73](src/limit/rules.ts:73)) and survives until browser restart. The user's escape hatch from a broken site is the Remove button, and pressing it makes the site stay broken. `clearEnforcement()` exists and has no caller. **High**

4. **The limit banner appears exactly once per site, ever.** `notice.js` is not a manifest content script ([public/manifest.json:41](public/manifest.json:41)); it exists in a page only because `announce()` injected it, and `announce` has one caller, inside `if (wanted !== enforcementFor(site))` ([src/limit/governor.ts:166](src/limit/governor.ts:166)). A reload or a second tab finds the tier unchanged and skips the block. So a site at `strict` — the terminal tier, where every subresource is refused — shows the explanation on the load that crossed 100% and never again. That is precisely the "reads as a broken website" failure the banner exists to prevent ([src/limit/notify.ts:1](src/limit/notify.ts:1), README.md:71-74). **High**

5. **No hosted privacy policy and no link to one from the product.** The policy exists only as a repo file. The Privacy & data panel is export/range/delete with no statement of what is stored ([src/settings.html:254](src/settings.html:254)); the manifest has no `homepage_url` and no `author`. Publish the policy at a stable URL, put it in the listing and in the manifest, and add a two-sentence summary plus link to the Privacy panel. Fix blocker 1 first — a reviewer who catches the retention discrepancy will discount the whole document. **Medium**

6. **The privacy policy says sync carries no browsing data; it carries site names.** Budgets are `chrome.storage.sync` and each carries a `site` ([src/limit/budgets.ts:243](src/limit/budgets.ts:243)); optimize settings sync the `exclusions` array of site keys ([src/optimize/features.ts:216](src/optimize/features.ts:216)). PRIVACY_POLICY.md:62-65 says the transfer "carries no browsing data". A list of domains someone capped and a list they excluded is the most opinionated slice of their browsing. Move both to `chrome.storage.local` with a one-time sync fallback read, or amend the sentence. **Medium**

7. **The project is not under version control.** `git rev-parse` reports no repository; `.gitignore` is carefully written and inert, so `node_modules/` (64 MB), `dist/`, `dist-throttle/` and `outputs/` are simply part of the folder. There is no history, no diff, no bisect for a measurement regression, and no place for CI to run. The defect archaeology embedded in test comments ([tests/reconcile.test.mjs:176](tests/reconcile.test.mjs:176), [scripts/smoke.mjs:89](scripts/smoke.mjs:89), [src/track/ledger.ts:473](src/track/ledger.ts:473)) is currently the project's only record of its own history. **High** (process, not store policy — but everything else is downstream of it)

8. **A typed limit is silently multiplied by ten in comma-decimal locales.** `parseByteSize` strips every comma before parsing ([src/core/format.ts:114](src/core/format.ts:114)), so `"1,5 GB"` is stored as 15 GB. The function's own docstring promises it returns `null` "rather than guessing at a limit that will later be enforced by cutting off someone's connection"; it guesses, wrong, by an order of magnitude. `settings.ts` never echoes the parsed size ([src/settings.ts:219](src/settings.ts:219)), so the misparse is invisible at entry and the limit simply never fires. **Medium**

## 3. Correctness & reliability

### Data integrity

| Defect | Where | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any unoptimized visit counts as a control | [src/track/tabs.ts:100](src/track/tabs.ts:100), [src/optimize/savings.ts:220](src/optimize/savings.ts:220) | **High** |
| Holdout tab set lost on worker restart | [src/optimize/holdout.ts:46](src/optimize/holdout.ts:46) | **Medium** |
| Failed flush discards buffered deltas permanently | [src/track/ledger.ts:352](src/track/ledger.ts:352) | **Medium** |
| Upload bytes vanish if the worker dies mid-request | [src/track/requests.ts:58](src/track/requests.ts:58) | **Medium** |
| Rejected rule install leaves the decision map lying | [src/limit/enforce.ts:111](src/limit/enforce.ts:111) | **Medium** |

**`Visit.optimized` is a bare boolean, so the control arm is contaminated three ways** ([src/core/types.ts:261](src/core/types.ts:261), set at [src/track/tabs.ts:100](src/track/tabs.ts:100)). `visitDelta` treats every falsy value as a control ([src/optimize/savings.ts:220](src/optimize/savings.ts:220)), which sweeps in (a) every load from before Data Saver was enabled — turn it on today and the previous 30 days become your control group, so the first "measured" saving is really before-vs-after-install; (b) loads on excluded sites; (c) loads where `applied()` was still `null` because `applyOptimize()` had not resolved. `GET_PAGE_FEATURES` guards that race with `await optimizeReady` ([src/background.ts:509](src/background.ts:509)); the resolver and `onBeforeNavigate` do not ([src/background.ts:85](src/background.ts:85), [src/background.ts:121](src/background.ts:121)). Since DNR session rules and the `persistAcrossSessions` script survive teardown, case (c) files genuinely-optimized loads as controls. Replace the boolean with `"optimized" | "holdout" | "disabled" | "excluded" | "unknown"`, count only `holdout` as control and only `optimized` as treatment, bump `DB_VERSION`, treat pre-migration rows as `unknown`.

**The holdout tab set has no `chrome.storage.session` mirror** ([src/optimize/holdout.ts:46](src/optimize/holdout.ts:46)) — unlike `tabs` ([src/track/tabs.ts:150](src/track/tabs.ts:150)) and `enforcement` ([src/limit/enforce.ts:39](src/limit/enforce.ts:39)), which both restore for exactly this reason. A control tab outlives the worker; the next wake republishes rules with `holdoutTabIds() === []` ([src/optimize/apply.ts:46](src/optimize/apply.ts:46)) and the tab is silently optimized while its `Visit.optimized` still reads `false`. The bias is one-directional: it drags `controlMean` toward `optimizedMean`, understating the one figure the README stakes credibility on.

**Beyond the control-arm problem, the holdout sampling is not random.** `decideHoldout` allows at most one control per site per day and holds out deterministically below `MIN_VISIT_SAMPLES` ([src/optimize/holdout.ts:145](src/optimize/holdout.ts:145), :150), so the first three controls per site are the *first eligible load of the day* — systematically heavier (cold heuristic cache, cold SW, auth bootstrap) for reasons the optimizer had nothing to do with. `visitDelta` compares raw means of a heavy-tailed distribution with no variance and no interval ([src/optimize/savings.ts:223](src/optimize/savings.ts:223)); PLAN.md:386 requires the report "show confidence, not just a number". Randomise within the day, record load-ordinal on `Visit` so like can be matched with like, use a trimmed mean, and return a spread alongside `savedPerVisit` — suppress the row when the interval straddles zero. (`savedTotal` is never rendered, so the extrapolation is at least not user-visible today.)

**A failed flush loses the deltas outright.** `doFlush` swaps `daily`/`hourly`/`hosts` into locals before the first await; a rejection is only `console.error`d ([src/track/ledger.ts:374](src/track/ledger.ts:374)) and the locals go out of scope. An aborted IndexedDB transaction means those bytes are gone with the only trace in a console nobody opens. Pair each task with its map, fold rejected maps back into the live buffer, `flushSoon()`, and record a `lastFlushError` that `GET_STORAGE_REPORT` can surface.

**Uploads are the one undisclosed undercount.** `requestUpBytes` folds in the declared `Content-Length` at `onSendHeaders` and stashes it in an in-memory map keyed by `requestId` ([src/track/requests.ts:58](src/track/requests.ts:58), [src/track/wire.ts:91](src/track/wire.ts:91)). A long upload fires no events in between, so a quiet 30 seconds kills the worker and a 500 MB upload is recorded with zero up-bytes. README.md:41-56 enumerates header halving, WebSocket frames, cancelled requests and cache hits; this is not among them. Persist entries above ~64 kB to `chrome.storage.session` and sweep stale ids in `maintenance()`.

### Attribution

- **Service-worker-initiated requests land in `#background`.** `attributeSite` returns `BACKGROUND_SITE` for every `tabId < 0` and consults `details.initiator` only to detect `chrome-extension://` ([src/track/wire.ts:142](src/track/wire.ts:142)). On a site whose SW re-issues fetches, those bytes miss the site row, miss `addTabBytes` (visitId is null — [src/track/requests.ts:97](src/track/requests.ts:97), [src/track/tabs.ts:232](src/track/tabs.ts:232)), and are fed to the governor as `noteUsage('#background', …)` — so a per-site budget never counts them. The README defence at [src/track/wire.ts:127](src/track/wire.ts:127) is about the request's *host*; for a tabId=-1 request the `initiator` is the *page origin*, which is the thing being asked for. Fall back to `siteKeyFromHost(hostFromUrl(details.initiator))` before returning `BACKGROUND_SITE`; browser-internal requests have no initiator and opaque origins give `"null"`, so both still land in `#background`. Requires changing the assertion at [tests/wire.test.mjs:125](tests/wire.test.mjs:125). **Medium**
- **Prerendered navigations are treated as real page loads.** Both webNavigation listeners filter only on `frameId` ([src/background.ts:119](src/background.ts:119), [src/background.ts:131](src/background.ts:131)) and nothing in `src/` reads `documentLifecycle` or `frameType`. A speculation-rules prerender under the host tab's id runs `noteNavigation`, which truncates the visit the user is actually looking at, installs a tab record for a site they have not visited, re-scopes enforcement onto it, and spends a holdout decision on it. Guard with `documentLifecycle !== "active" || frameType !== "outermost_frame"`, falling back to the `frameId` test on older Chrome. **Medium**
- **Navigating away from a limited site blocks the next site's subresources.** The tab-scoped rule has no domain condition — it blocks the listed types for *every* request from that tab ([src/limit/rules.ts:66](src/limit/rules.ts:66)) — and is only re-scoped after `onCommitted` → `noteNavigation(...).then(syncEnforcementTabs)` ([src/background.ts:133](src/background.ts:133)), which awaits `ensureTabsReady()` and then `finishVisit`'s IndexedDB write ([src/track/tabs.ts:267](src/track/tabs.ts:267)) before `refreshEnforcementTabs` even starts its own round trip. `main_frame` is never blocked, so the next site's document arrives while its scripts and CSS are refused — with no banner, since the notice died with the old document. Drop the tab from the rules at `onBeforeNavigate` (the listener already exists at [src/background.ts:119](src/background.ts:119)) when the target site differs, and stop making `syncEnforcementTabs` wait on the visit write. **Medium**

### Rules & enforcement

- **Optimize redirects outrank limit blocks.** Limit rules are `priority: 1` ([src/limit/rules.ts:69](src/limit/rules.ts:69), :77); pack redirects are `priority: 2` ([src/optimize/rules.ts:28](src/optimize/rules.ts:28)). DNR compares priority first and uses the allow > block > redirect ordering only as a tie-break *within* a priority — so the comments at [src/optimize/rules.ts:21](src/optimize/rules.ts:21) and [src/rules/session.ts:15](src/rules/session.ts:15) state the rule backwards, and a hard cap on a site whose images come from a pack CDN keeps spending bytes after 100%. Move limits to `priority: 3` and add a `tests/rules.test.mjs` assertion that `min(limit) > max(optimize)`. **Medium**
- **A rejected install leaves `entries` claiming a tier Chrome is not applying.** `setEnforcement` writes the tier before awaiting `syncRules()` ([src/limit/enforce.ts:111](src/limit/enforce.ts:111)); `install()` rethrows ([src/rules/session.ts:98](src/rules/session.ts:98)); `persist()` is skipped; the throw escapes `syncEnforcement`'s unguarded `for` loop so remaining sites are never evaluated; and the caller is a fire-and-forget microtask ([src/limit/governor.ts:132](src/limit/governor.ts:132)). From then on `noteUsage`'s `if (wanted === enforcementFor(site)) return` never fires again for that site. Snapshot and roll back on failure, wrap the loop body in try/catch, and validate site keys before building rules the way `#`-prefixed buckets are rejected at [src/limit/rules.ts:64](src/limit/rules.ts:64). **Medium**
- **One failed rule install poisons the governor for the worker's life.** `ensureReady()` memoizes `reloadBudgets()` with no catch ([src/limit/governor.ts:83](src/limit/governor.ts:83)), so a rejection makes `budgetStatuses()`, `refreshWindows()` and `currentPeriodKey()` throw until either a budget edit reassigns `ready` ([src/limit/governor.ts:237](src/limit/governor.ts:237)) or the worker dies. Every other memoized readiness in the tree swallows deliberately ([src/track/estimate.ts:84](src/track/estimate.ts:84) even documents the hazard). Clear the memo on failure. **Low**
- **Limit and exclusion forms accept non-domains and create dead limits.** The only validation is `siteKeyFromHost(input) !== ""` ([src/settings.ts:199](src/settings.ts:199)). `"YouTube"` → `"youtube"` (no public suffix, [src/core/sites.ts:128](src/core/sites.ts:128)); a pasted `https://youtube.com/watch` trips the IPv6 colon guard at [src/core/sites.ts:130](src/core/sites.ts:130) and the whole URL becomes the site key. Either way the form says "Daily limit saved.", a row appears at 0%, and the budget can never match a real site. Same hole in the exclusion form ([src/settings.ts:306](src/settings.ts:306)). **Medium**

### Measurement accuracy

| Defect | Where | Effect | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Cookie`/`Set-Cookie` invisible without `extraHeaders` | [src/track/requests.ts:112](src/track/requests.ts:112), :123 | `up` structurally low; undisclosed | **Medium** |
| HEAD `Content-Length` counted as body *and* trained on | [src/track/requests.ts:200](src/track/requests.ts:200) | One HEAD can define a cold `host\|type` mean | **Low** |
| Header bytes added twice on every estimate | [src/track/reconcile.ts:179](src/track/reconcile.ts:179) | Estimated requests inflated by one halved header block | **Low** |
| DST fall-back merges two hours; spring-forward leaves a gap | [src/core/period.ts:71](src/core/period.ts:71) | Two days a year, hourly chart only | **Low** |
| Backwards clock step makes parked entries un-expirable | [src/track/reconcile.ts:231](src/track/reconcile.ts:231) | Latent; `forgetTab`/`drainPending`/`MAX_PENDING` still escape | **Low** |
| Modelled savings reach CSV as raw floats | [src/track/stats.ts:454](src/track/stats.ts:454) | `1234.5600000000002` in the one artefact users check | **Low** |

The `extraHeaders` one deserves a sentence beyond the table: the README's "counted from the names and values, then halved" implies halving is the only source of error, when the input set is incomplete before the halving — a logged-in session's `Cookie` header is routinely a few kB and is counted as zero. Either add `"extraHeaders"` and measure the cost of the optimizations it disables, or name it in "What is approximate" next to the halving note. The whole credibility argument of this extension is that it names its own error bars.

`priceCompleted` tests `contentLength()` before `bodyImpossible()` ([src/track/requests.ts:200](src/track/requests.ts:200)), so a HEAD probe against a 60 MB file books 60 MB of `down` *and* calls `sizeModel.observe` with it — and on a cold key [src/track/estimate.ts:123](src/track/estimate.ts:123) sets `mean = bytes` with no outlier clamp, so one HEAD defines the mean that prices every blocked request and cache hit on that host. `tests/wire.test.mjs` pins `bodyImpossible(200, "HEAD") === true` while the predicate is never consulted. Swap the order.

The double-count is a convention conflict: the model is trained on header-inclusive figures ([src/track/requests.ts:203](src/track/requests.ts:203) passes `headerDown + body`, [src/track/reconcile.ts:206](src/track/reconcile.ts:206) observes `transferSize`), but `commitEstimate` sets `down = headerBytes + estimate` ([src/track/reconcile.ts:179](src/track/reconcile.ts:179)). Pick one convention, hold it, and say which in the `estimate.test.mjs` docstring.

### Packs

- **Cloudinary rasterises SVGs and re-encodes GIFs.** `^(https://res\.cloudinary\.com/[^/]+/image/upload/)(v\d+/.+)$` ([src/optimize/packs.ts:122](src/optimize/packs.ts:122)) has no extension filter, and Cloudinary delivers SVG untouched only while *no* transformation is applied. A 6 kB vector logo becomes a raster that is often larger, stops scaling on HiDPI, and breaks `mask-image` / `currentColor` / sprite references. Require `\.(?:jpe?g|png|webp)(?:[?#].*)?$` and add must-not-match tests for `.svg`, `.gif`, `.ico`, `.pdf`. The pack's four stated safety properties do not include "the transformation is appropriate for the media type" ([src/optimize/packs.ts:10](src/optimize/packs.ts:10)) — they should. **Medium**
- **Photon rewrites `w=` when `resize`/`fit`/`crop` makes it inert.** `?resize=1600%2C900&w=1600&ssl=1` matches ([src/optimize/packs.ts:96](src/optimize/packs.ts:96)); Photon resolves `resize` first, so the response is still 1600px — but the URL changed, so it is a redirect and a cache miss, and `creditRewrite` books `actualBytes / 0.35 - actualBytes` ≈ 1.86× as saved ([src/optimize/savings.ts:104](src/optimize/savings.ts:104)). Conversely `?fit=…` with no `w=` is missed entirely. **Low**
- **Photon's description promises compression it does not request.** "and asks for slightly stronger compression" against `regexSubstitution: \1${TARGET_WIDTH}\2` ([src/optimize/packs.ts:93](src/optimize/packs.ts:93)). Fix the copy, and add a test asserting each description mentions only parameters that appear in its substitution — the other four pack tests exist to prevent exactly this class of drift. **Low**
- **`TARGET_WIDTH` is a fixed 800 justified by phone viewports, in a desktop-only extension.** `devicePixelRatio` appears nowhere in `src/`. Wikimedia's `\d{4,}` floor fires on 1024px, and on a 2× display an 800-CSS-px lead image selects the 1600px candidate, which the pack rewrites to 800 and the browser upscales. Rewriting also changes `naturalWidth`, which MediaWiki's Media Viewer computes from. Read DPR and screen width once at startup, pick from an 800/1200/1600 ladder, and rebuild rules on change — rules are already rebuilt on every settings change. At minimum raise the Wikimedia floor above 1200 and state the resolution tradeoff in the description; it currently sits under README.md:111's claim that packs remove bytes "without removing anything you would see". **Medium**
- **Shopify covers only the legacy filename form.** `_2048x2048.jpg?v=1` matches; `?v=1&width=1946` (what modern themes emit via `image_url`), `_2048x2048_crop_center.jpg` and `_1024x1024@2x.jpg` all miss ([src/optimize/packs.ts:107](src/optimize/packs.ts:107)). **Medium**
- **Pack regexes are case-sensitive in JS and case-insensitive in Chrome.** `RuleCondition` never sets `isUrlFilterCaseSensitive` ([src/rules/session.ts:28](src/rules/session.ts:28)), which defaults to false, while `isRewritable`/`packForUrl`/`applyPack` use no `i` flag — so `.../1600PX-Ex.JPG` is rewritten by Chrome and unattributed by `packForRedirect`, and its original size is never banked. The "must not match its own output" test also runs under semantics Chrome does not use. Add the flag, or set the condition explicitly. **Low**

### Page optimizers

- **`clickToLoadMedia` permanently kills MSE players.** `gateMedia` guards `srcObject` but not the src scheme ([src/content/optimize.ts:217](src/content/optimize.ts:217)); `holdMediaSources` removes the `src` attribute ([src/content/optimize.ts:129](src/content/optimize.ts:129)) and `gateMedia` then calls `media.load()` ([src/content/optimize.ts:255](src/content/optimize.ts:255)). Every hls.js / dash.js / Shaka / video.js player assigns `URL.createObjectURL(mediaSource)` to `video.src`, which reflects to the attribute — so it is caught at attach time (paused, `currentTime` 0), the MediaSource transitions to `closed`, and `restoreAttribute` puts back a dead blob URL. "Load video" yields a permanently broken player, not a paused one; the `try/catch` at :254 does not help because `load()` succeeds destructively. Return early on `/^(?:blob|data|mediastream):/i`. Off by default behind a default-off master switch, which is the only thing bounding this. **Medium**
- **`trimSrcset` can trigger a second download.** It sets `image.sizes` with no `complete`/`currentSrc` guard ([src/content/optimize.ts:66](src/content/optimize.ts:66)), and `start()` sweeps the whole document on `load` ([src/content/optimize.ts:365](src/content/optimize.ts:365)) — after every parser-inserted `img[srcset]` has completed. Mutating `sizes` re-runs image-source selection; a newly-selected candidate not in the memory cache starts a second fetch, so the default-on feature can cost large + small. The `dataset.byteBudgetSized` flag limits the exposure to images that returned early on `width <= 0` plus anything inserted after load. Bail on `image.complete || image.currentSrc`. **Medium**
- **`blockBeacons` blocks all of `sendBeacon`, under an "invisible" label.** Unscoped `{type: "block"}` on `resourceTypes: ["ping"]`, default on ([src/optimize/rules.ts:99](src/optimize/rules.ts:99), [src/optimize/features.ts:61](src/optimize/features.ts:61)). `sendBeacon` is the standard way to flush unsaved editor state on `pagehide`, post CSP/error reports, and notify session termination — the description's "nothing on the page waits for them" is true of the network wait and false of the consequence. And it cannot be switched off. Scope by destination with a `requestDomains` analytics list (same shape as the pack list, same "matches what it claims / matches nothing else" tests), or retitle it and expose the toggle. **Medium**
- **Baselines and rewrite credits are skipped for cached responses.** `creditOptimizer` is only reached from the `contentLength !== null` branch ([src/track/requests.ts:204](src/track/requests.ts:204)); the `fromCache` path returns at :191. So a control load — the deliberately expensive thing whose entire purpose is recording what the original variant weighs — banks nothing for any image served from cache, which on a repeat visit is most of them. Chrome supplies `responseHeaders` for cache hits, so the original's declared size is knowable without counting a byte. Call `observeBaseline` on that path, guarded by `contentLength !== null`. **Medium**
- **Any extension's block is credited to Byte Budget.** `isRefusedByOptimizer(type)` tests a global type set ([src/optimize/savings.ts:261](src/optimize/savings.ts:261)) while its sibling at the same call site is site-scoped `isEnforcedByUs(entry.site, type)` — with the comment immediately above stating that "neither should be credited for the other's work — or for an ad blocker's" ([src/track/requests.ts:284](src/track/requests.ts:284)). Give it the same signature and return false for excluded sites and holdout tabs. **Low**

### Smaller

- `openDb` wires no `onblocked` handler and caches the promise ([src/core/db.ts:72](src/core/db.ts:72)), so a blocked upgrade leaves every later call awaiting forever. Cannot fire at `DB_VERSION 1`; goes live the moment it is bumped. `upgrade()` also has no `oldVersion` branching, so a future migration has nowhere to live. **Low**
- Alarms are asserted only on `onInstalled`/`onStartup`, both `void`ed with no catch ([src/background.ts:179](src/background.ts:179), :190). Assert them idempotently at module top level. **Low**

Add `const unhandled: never = request; throw …`. **Low**
- Session budgets: `periodKeyFor("session")` returns a constant, `grantBytes` carries the old value forward, and `refreshWindows` can never see a key change ([src/limit/budgets.ts:121](src/limit/budgets.ts:121), :306), so grants compound forever. Unreachable today — both `PUT_BUDGET` call sites hardcode `"day"` — but a trap that arms the moment period selection ships. **Low**
- Throttle channel: `attached` is a global `Map<tabId, …>` with no site key and `void site` discards the disambiguator ([src/limit/throttle.ts:82](src/limit/throttle.ts:82)), so with two budgeted sites each `syncEnforcement` pass detaches the other's tabs — an attach/detach cycle every 60 seconds, flashing Chrome's un-suppressible debugging banner. No `chrome.debugger.onDetach` listener exists either, so an external detach leaves stale state and the cap never returns. **Medium** (within that channel)

## 5. Security, privacy & store policy

Blockers 1, 5 and 6 above are the load-bearing ones. Beyond them:

- **`Save-Data: on` is a fingerprinting bit, and the policy's "No new information about you is sent" is wrong.** One unscoped `modifyHeaders` rule sets it on seven resource types for every site minus the exclusion list ([src/optimize/rules.ts:88](src/optimize/rules.ts:88)). Chrome retired Lite mode in 100 and desktop Chrome has no UI to enable it, so in 2026 a desktop Chrome sending `Save-Data: on` is a rare, stable cross-site signal worth more to a tracker than the User-Agent. It is on by default and cannot be switched off. PRIVACY_POLICY.md:44-51 does disclose the header and what it signals — the failing clause is that one sentence. Give it a toggle, default it off (packs deliver the image savings deterministically; Save-Data only helps where honoured), and rewrite the clause to say plainly that switching it on makes the browser more distinguishable. **Medium**
- **CSP omits `default-src`, so the enforcement claim is a promise.** The policy is `script-src 'self'; object-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'` ([public/manifest.json:23](public/manifest.json:23)) — with no `default-src`, `img-src`, `media-src`, `style-src`, `font-src` and `frame-src` are unrestricted and `form-action`/`base-uri` (which never inherit) are unset. README.md:156-158 and PRIVACY_POLICY.md:58-60 both present `connect-src` as enforcement of "no network request of the extension's own"; it does not see `new Image().src`, a form submission or a prefetch link. There is no injection sink today — every extension page builds DOM through `element()`/`textContent` with no `innerHTML` — so this is defence in depth, but the claim is load-bearing in two user-facing documents. Tighten to `default-src 'self'; … img-src 'self' data:; style-src 'self'; form-action 'none'; base-uri 'none'` and verify the blob download at [src/settings.ts:426](src/settings.ts:426) still works. **Low**
- **Baselines store full third-party image URLs forever, outside retention.** The primary key is the complete request URL ([src/core/db.ts:62](src/core/db.ts:62), [src/optimize/savings.ts:145](src/optimize/savings.ts:145)) — `pbs.twimg.com/media/<mediaId>`, `res.cloudinary.com/<cloudName>/…` — and `pruneBaselines` caps by row count only, with `pruneOldRows` not touching the store at all. `readBaseline` only ever does exact-key `get`, so plaintext is never needed. Key on a SHA-256 hex digest and add an age bound using the `updatedAt` the rows already carry. Note that `observeBaseline` runs whenever `isRewritable(url)` regardless of whether Data Saver is on ([src/track/requests.ts:241](src/track/requests.ts:241)), so every install accumulates these. **Low**
- **CSV export has no formula-injection guard.** `csvCell` quotes on `"`, `,` and newline only ([src/track/stats.ts:387](src/track/stats.ts:387)); a leading `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or CR starts a formula in Excel, LibreOffice and Sheets even inside a quoted field. The `site` column comes straight from `hostFromUrl` with no character validation, and the WHATWG parser accepts `=` and `+` in an http host (`new URL("http://=cmd/").hostname === "=cmd"`). Prefix-escape in `csvCell`; separately reject site keys outside `[a-z0-9.:_-]` in `siteKeyFromHost` so garbage cannot become a row label anywhere. **Low**
- **The page optimizer marks the page's own DOM.** `image.dataset.byteBudgetSized` ([src/content/optimize.ts:67](src/content/optimize.ts:67)) is written into the shared DOM, unlike the `__byteBudgetOptimize` window marker in the isolated world. Any page can pre-set `data-byte-budget-sized="1"` at render time and silently disable `trimSrcset` on every image, with no signal anywhere. `allowedMedia` next door is already a `WeakSet` in the same IIFE — use one here. (The fingerprinting half is weak: `image.sizes` mutation is observable regardless.) **Low**
- **`notice.js` listens for a window event any page can fire.** `window.addEventListener("byte-budget-refresh", refresh)` ([src/content/notice.ts:204](src/content/notice.ts:204)) is reachable from the page's main world, so a site the user is over budget on can drive unbounded `GET_TAB_NOTICE` messages, each keeping the worker awake and rebuilding the banner's shadow DOM. Drop the event: `announce()` only injects after `tabs.sendMessage` failed, so following the injection with a `sendMessage` collapses the case into the ordinary `NOTICE_UPDATE` path. **Low**

## 7. Accessibility & internationalization

- **Three tablists claim ARIA tab semantics and implement none of it.** The popup period tabs and both dashboard tablists set `role="tab"`/`aria-selected` inside `role="tablist"` ([src/popup.ts:81](src/popup.ts:81), [src/dashboard.ts:136](src/dashboard.ts:136), :154) with no keydown handler, no roving `tabIndex`, no `aria-controls` and no `role="tabpanel"` anywhere in the three HTML files. A screen reader announces "Today tab, selected, 2 of 4"; the user presses Left and nothing moves. The theme picker does all of this correctly ([src/settings.ts:328](src/settings.ts:328)) with identical `.segmented` styling, which is what makes it a slip. There is no tabpanel here — changing period re-renders the surface — so convert all three to `role="radiogroup"`/`role="radio"` and lift `bindGroup`/`paintGroup` into `core/dom.ts`. That deletes the ad-hoc tab wiring and gets arrow keys, Home/End and roving tabindex for all three at once. **Medium**
- **`--text-muted` fails AA in the light theme, at 9–12px, everywhere.** `#6d8087` measures 4.13:1 on `#ffffff`, 3.79:1 on `--bg`, 3.71:1 on `--surface-2` ([src/app.css:26](src/app.css:26)) — all under 4.5:1, and nothing carrying it is large enough for the 3:1 exemption (`.chart-bar-tick` 9px, `.impact-label` 9px, `.site-share` 10px, `.panel-note`/`.stat-hint`/`.field-hint`/`.save-status` 11px). Dark is fine at 6.11:1, so this is light-theme-only. Darken to ~`#5a6f76`, which clears all three while staying subordinate to `--text-soft`. Also `--estimate` `#a95d10` on `--estimate-soft` measures 4.40:1 at 11px ([src/dashboard.css:519](src/dashboard.css:519)) — nudge to ~`#9c5406`. Add a contrast assertion over the token pairs in `tests/` so the palette cannot drift back. **Medium**
- **Opening and closing the site detail panel moves no focus and announces nothing.** `selectSite` un-hides, fills and smooth-scrolls with focus left on the row ([src/dashboard.ts:310](src/dashboard.ts:310)); `liveRegion` is written only on error paths. Closing is worse: `detailPanel.hidden = true` fires while focus is inside it ([src/dashboard.ts:367](src/dashboard.ts:367)), so Chrome drops the user to `<body>` at the top of the document with no route back. The panel already carries `aria-labelledby="detail-title"`, so `tabindex="-1"` + `focus({preventScroll: true})` on open and a saved row reference on close is a two-line fix. **Medium**
- **The in-page limit notice is inserted already-populated, so it is never announced.** `render()` sets `role="status"`, fills the headline and detail, and only then appends the host ([src/content/notice.ts:117](src/content/notice.ts:117), :187). A live region must exist in the tree before its contents change. So the one mechanism separating "a limit I set" from "a broken website" is silent for the users with the least alternative signal — a sighted user at least sees the gap where an image was. Attach an empty card first, fill on the next frame, and keep it empty rather than absent between renders so re-renders on tier change also announce. **Low**
- **Dismissing the notice silences it permanently for that document.** `dismissed` is set at [src/content/notice.ts:140](src/content/notice.ts:140), checked at :104, and never reset — not on `NOTICE_UPDATE`, not on removal. A user who dismisses the `trim` banner gets nothing when the page later escalates to `strict`, which is the tier where the page is genuinely broken and on a long-lived tab is exactly where the explanation matters most. Store `dismissedTier` and re-render on a heavier tier; the tiers are already ordered. **Low**
- **Heading and landmark structure.** `popup.html` has no `h1` — the period total is a `<p class="headline-total">` and the first heading is an `h2` — and its `<main>` wraps only the Sites panel, so "skip to main content" lands past everything else. `dashboard.html` has no `<main>` at all; only `settings.html` does. All three are tag-only changes; `.page` and `.app` select children by class. **Low**
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured in the content script and nowhere else.** [src/content/notice.ts:92](src/content/notice.ts:92) wraps its entrance animation correctly, so the pattern is understood — but `html { scroll-behavior: smooth }` is global ([src/dashboard.css:3](src/dashboard.css:3)) and `detailPanel.scrollIntoView({behavior: "smooth"})` fires on an ordinary click ([src/dashboard.ts:362](src/dashboard.ts:362)). Add a global reduce block to `app.css` and pick the `scrollIntoView` behaviour from `matchMedia` — the CSS override does not reach the option. **Low**
- **Two dismiss buttons are under 24×24.** The exclusion chip's remove button is `18px` square with `padding: 0` in a `gap: 6px` wrap flex, so the SC 2.5.8 spacing exception is unavailable ([src/dashboard.css:831](src/dashboard.css:831)); the notice's close is `padding: 0 5px; font-size: 15px; line-height: 1`, roughly 15px tall ([src/content/notice.ts:84](src/content/notice.ts:84)). Both are the sole undo for their action, and the notice one lands on arbitrary third-party pages where a touch user has no alternative. **Low**
